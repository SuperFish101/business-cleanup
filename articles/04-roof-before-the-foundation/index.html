The AI bros are selling you the roof before the foundation

You are being sold AI right now. Agents that answer your leads. Bots that follow up. Tools that run your business while you sleep. New one every week, and a guy on the internet swearing it changed his life.

Here is the part none of them will tell you.

If your business is a mess, AI will not fix it. It will just make a faster mess.

I am not anti-AI. I use it every day. But I have watched the order everyone does this in, and the order is backwards. People buy the tool first and fix the business never. Then they wonder why the magic did not happen.

Let me tell you why it did not.

An AI in a messy business is a smart new hire with no training

Here is the whole problem in one line.

An AI dropped into your business is just a very smart person on their first day, with no training, guessing at how you do things.

Think about what happens when you hire someone genuinely sharp and give them no instructions. They are smart. They want to do well. And they still get it wrong for weeks. Not because they are bad, but because being smart is not the same as knowing how your business works.

They do not know that this client gets a phone call and that one gets a text. They do not know you never send a quote before you have seen the place. They do not know the thing you have never said out loud, because to you it is obvious.

AI is that person. Faster, cheaper, awake at two in the morning, and just as clueless about how you actually work.

So when you point it at a business that has never been written down, you do not get a great employee. You get confident nonsense. It sounds right. It is wrong in ways you will not catch until a client does. And because it is fast, it produces that nonsense at a speed no human ever could. That is the faster mess.

More tools is the wrong move

When things get chaotic, the instinct is to reach for something new. Another app. Another subscription. The AI everyone is posting about.

It almost never helps, because chaos is not a tool problem. It is a "this all lives in my head" problem. Adding a tool to a business that runs on memory and a group chat just gives you one more place for things to slip.

The AI bros are selling you the roof before you have poured the foundation. A roof is great. On top of a foundation. Sitting in the mud, it is just an expensive thing in the mud.

And this is not only a small-business mistake. A company called Jasper raised 125 million dollars building its whole business as a friendly layer on top of one AI tool. Weeks later that tool, ChatGPT, started doing the same job for free, and within months Jasper was cutting staff. They built an expensive roof on ground they did not own, so when the ground moved there was nothing left underneath. The lesson scales down: build the part you actually own, not a layer that lives or dies by someone else's tool.

So what is the foundation?

The boring fix nobody can sell you

The thing that makes AI genuinely useful is not a smarter model or a cleverer tool.

It is a business that has been written down properly.

Not a binder. Not a policy manual nobody opens. I mean the real thing. How work actually moves through your company. What happens when a lead comes in. Who touches it next. What "done right" looks like at each step. The rules you keep in your head that have never made it onto paper.

Once that exists, everything changes. The same AI that was guessing now has the answers in front of it. It stops inventing and starts following. It is not a smart stranger anymore. It is someone who read the manual before their first shift.

That is the whole trick. There is nothing clever about it, which is exactly why nobody runs ads for it. No one can charge you a monthly subscription for "go write down how your business works."

And here is the quiet bonus. A written-down business does not just help the AI. It helps the next person you hire, the contractor you bring on, the day you want to take off. The AI is just the newest thing that can finally read it.

What "written down" actually looks like

Let us make it concrete, because "write your business down" is easy to say and vague to picture.

Take one job you do over and over. Following up on quotes. Reminding a client about an appointment. Answering a new lead. Pick the one that annoys you most because it always slips.

Written down, that one job is just a small folder with a few plain files in it. What the job is and the rules for doing it. Where each open item stands right now. The actual wording you use. That is it. You, taking a thing you already do, and putting it into a few short files instead of keeping it in your head.

Now you open ChatGPT or Claude, point it at that folder, and ask it to do the follow-up pass. It reads what the job is, checks where things stand, drafts the messages in your words, and hands them to you to approve. It is not guessing anymore. You wrote down the answers.

Do that for one job and you feel it. Do it for the fifteen jobs that make up your whole business, and the business finally lives somewhere other than your memory.

None of this is my invention, by the way. A man named Jake Van Clief wrote it up properly and gave it a name. All the name means is this. Keep your knowledge in clear, plain files, and one AI reading the right file at the right moment can do work people normally build complicated software for. He teaches the whole thing free, and I will point you to him at the end.

Try it this week

Here is something you can actually do, in about twenty minutes, with nothing but a notes app.

Pick the one repetitive job that annoys you most. Write it down the way you would explain it to a new hire. What the job is, when it happens, the rules, and the one or two things people always get wrong. Then jot down what is open right now, and paste in a real example of the message you send.

Then open ChatGPT or Claude, give it those notes, and ask it to draft the next round for you to approve.

You will see the difference immediately. It will sound like you, because you wrote down your voice. It will chase the right things, because you wrote down what is open. It will stop making things up, because you finally gave it the answers.

That is the entire idea, working, in one folder. The only thing left after that is doing it for the rest of the business.

For anyone who wants to check the thinking

I am not asking you to take my word for it. The companies building the AI everyone is talking about chose this exact approach for their own tools. Anthropic's coding assistant reads a plain markdown file for its instructions and stores what it needs to remember in ordinary files. OpenAI's coding tool does the same. When the people who build these models needed a way to tell an AI how to do a job, they did not build a database or an app. They wrote it down in plain files in folders. The same thing I am describing here.

There is also a paper on it, "Interpretable Context Methodology: Folder Structure as Agentic Architecture" by Jake Van Clief and David McDermott, published March 2026, if you like to read the source.

I mention this so a skeptic who goes looking finds solid ground. It is not the reason to do it. The reason to do it is that your business stops living in your head.
